WHEAT: The outlook for 2026/27 U.S. wheat this month is for higher supplies, increased 
domestic use, reduced exports, and greater ending stocks . Supplies are raised by 8 million 
bushels on higher imports and production. The NASS Small Grains Annual Summary 
estimated wheat production slightly greater at 1,534 million bushels on an increased yield 
more than offsetting reduced harvested area. Despite the increase, this remains the smallest 
U.S. wheat production since 1970 . Domestic use is raised 10 million bushels, all on higher 
feed and residual use . The NASS Grain Stocks report implied larger -than-previously-
expected first quarter (June- August) domestic disappearance. Exports are reduced 25 million 
bushels to 750 million on lower sales and shipments to date for Hard Red Winter (HRW) and 
Hard Red Spring that are partially offset by increased White exports . HRW is the largest 
decline as HRW continues to be uncompetitive in export markets with significantly higher 
prices than last year. Projected all wheat ending stocks are raised by 23 million bushels to 
740 million but are still 20 percent below last year . The season-average farm price is lowered 
$0.10 per bushel to $6.30 on increased stocks, NASS prices reported to date, and 
expectations for futures and cash prices for the remainder of the marketing year.  
This month’s 2026/27 global wheat outlook is for higher supplies, greater consumption, 
reduced trade, and slightly lower ending stocks . Supplies are projected up 0.9 million tons to 
1,103.9 million on increased beginning stocks mainly for Australia. Global production is only 
minimally revised with offsetting changes for several countries . EU production is lowered 0.8 
million tons to 133.0 million, mostly on a reduction for Germany . Canada and Turkey 
production are each raised 0.5 million tons to 36.5 million and 23.0 million, respectively , on 
higher yields. Global consumption is raised 1.2 million tons to 827.9 million, primarily on 
higher feed and residual use for China and the EU . World trade is 2.8 million tons lower at 
209.0 million as reductions for Russia and the United States are only partially offset by 
increased exports for Argentina and Canada . Russia’s exports are now the lowest in 5 years 
at 40.0 million tons with logistical disruptions caused by the continu ing conflict in the Black 
Sea region reducing shipments to date well below their long- term average. Projected 
2026/27 global ending stocks are fractionally lower at 276.0 million tons with decreases for 
the EU, China, Argentina, Egypt, and several other countries nearly offset by increases for 
Russia, Australia, and the United States.  
COARSE GRAINS: This month’s 2026/27 U.S. corn outlook is for larger supplies, exports, 
domestic use, and ending stocks. Projected beginning stocks for 2026/27 are raised 173 
million bushels based on the September 30 Grain Stocks  report. Corn production is higher as 
well, up 234 million bushels to 16.0 billion on a 2.7- bushel increase in yield to 181.2 bushels 
per acre. Harvested area for grain is unchanged at 88.5 million acres. Total use is increased 
125 million bushels to 16.3 billion . Each component of use is increased. Exports are raised
