WHEAT: The outlook for 2026/27 U.S. wheat this month is for unchanged supplies, slightly lower 
domestic use, steady exports, and modestly higher ending stocks . Domestic use is reduced by 3 
million bushels, all on lower food use . The NASS Flour Milling Products  report indicated a 2 
percent year-to-year decline in wheat ground for flour in the July through September quarter . 
Total wheat exports are unchanged at 750 million bushels , but there are offsetting by -class 
changes for White and Hard Red Winter (HRW) . White wheat exports are raised on continued 
strong sales and shipments with White wheat priced competitively  against other exporters to 
Asian destinations . Conversely, HRW exports are lowered on continued weak sales and 
shipments as HRW exports are now the second lowest on record. Projected all wheat ending 
stocks are raised by 3 million bushels to 743 million but are still 19 percent below last year . The 
season-average farm price is lowered $0.10 per bushel to $6.20 on NASS prices reported to date 
and expectations for futures and cash prices for the remainder of the marketing year.  
This month’s 2026/27 global wheat outlook is for higher supplies, greater consumption, modestly 
lower trade, and larger stocks . Supplies are projected up 6.4 million tons to 1,110.4 million, 
primarily on higher production for Argentina, Australia, Kazakhstan, and Ukraine. Argentina and 
Australia are both raised 1.5 million tons to 23.0 and 32.5 million, respectively , on continued 
widespread favorable conditions as both countries now move into their harvests . Global 
consumption is raised 1.7 million tons to 829.6 million, mostly on higher feed and residual use for 
Australia, Kazakhstan, and Ukraine more than offsetting lower food, seed, and industrial use for 
Turkey. World trade is 0.3 million tons lower at 208.7 million as reduced exports for Russia and 
Ukraine more than offset increases for Australia and Argentina. Russia and Ukraine exports are 
each lowered 1.0 million tons to 39.0 and 11.5 million, respectively . The conflict in the Black Sea 
region continues to reduce shipments to date for both countries below their long-term averages 
despite efforts to utilize alternative ports and transportation modes . Projected 2026/27 global 
ending stocks are 4.8 million tons higher at 280.8 million tons, largely on increases for Ukraine, 
Argentina, Russia, and Kazakhstan.  
COARSE GRAINS: This month’s 2026/27 U.S. corn outlook is for smaller production, domestic 
use, exports, and ending stocks. Corn production is forecast at 15.9 billion bushels, down 141 
million from last month with a 1.6- bushel reduction in yield to 179.6 bushels per acre. Harvested 
area for grain remains at 88.5 million acres. With a smaller crop, feed and residual use is lowered 
25 million bushels to 6.0 billion. Exports are also lowered this month, down 25 million bushels to 
3.3 billion reflecting shipments  and sales to date. Corn ending stocks are reduced to 1.8 billion 
bushels. The season- average corn price received by producers is lowered $0.10 per bushel to 
$4.60.
