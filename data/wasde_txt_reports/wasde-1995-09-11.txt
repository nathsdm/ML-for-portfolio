COTTON HIGHLIGHTS

This month's U.S. cotton outlook for 1995/96 features a
downward revision in stocks stemming from smaller
production and larger use.  Ending stocks are projected
at 3.7 million bales, nearly 30 percent below a month
earlier.  Smaller output is mainly responsible as the
crop forecast of 20.3 million bales is 7 percent below
August indications.  While prospective mill use remains
unchanged at 11.5 million bales, exports are revised up
1 percent to 7.8 million.

Smaller production and larger use also highlight this
month's revisions in world cotton prospects for
1995/96.  Global output is projected at 88.8 million
bales, 2 percent below a month earlier, reflecting
downward adjustments for the United States, China and
Australia.  Meanwhile, world consumption is revised up
slightly to a near-record 86.8 million bales, thanks
mainly to a more optimistic outlook for Turkey.  Global
trade prospects remain about unchanged.  This season's
ending stocks are placed at 31.7 million bales, 7
percent below August indications but 7 percent above
the beginning level.
