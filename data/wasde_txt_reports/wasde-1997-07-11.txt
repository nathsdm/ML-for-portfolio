WASDE-328 - July 11, 1997 

NOTE:  This report adopts U.S. yield and production forecasts for
winter wheat, durum, other spring wheat, barley, and oats released
today by the National Agricultural Statistics Service (NASS).  Yields
for other crops reflect time series analysis and judgment.  Area
estimates reflect the June 30 NASS Acreage report.  Survey-based
area, yield, and production forecasts reported by NASS will be
adopted in the August 12 issue of this report. 
WHEAT:  Projected U.S. 1997/98 ending stocks are up 71 million
bushels from last month as a 127-million-bushel rise in the crop more
than offsets expanding use.  Winter wheat production is forecast up
177 million bushels from last month because of higher area and yields. 
The forecast for spring wheat (including durum), however, is lower. 
Also, with the U.S. durum crop down 30 percent from last year,
projected imports are up 10 million bushels from last month.  Partially
offsetting the larger all-wheat supplies are lower reported carryin
stocks than forecast last month, and a rise of 25 million bushels each
for domestic use and exports.  Still, ending stocks are substantially
higher and the projected price range is reduced 35 cents on each end to
$3.10 to $3.70 per bushel.

Projected 1997/98 global supply, use, and ending stocks are higher
than last month, but trade is down.  Larger prospective crops in the
United States, India, Pakistan, the EU, Russia, and Uzbekistan more
than offset reductions for Canada, Argentina, and Romania.  Smaller
imports for India, Russia, and Uzbekistan are largely offset by
increases for Egypt, Sri Lanka, and a number of other countries. 
Prospective exports are lower for all the major foreign exporting
countries, while U.S. exports are forecast higher.  Most of the rise in
prospective global ending stocks is in the United States, Pakistan,
India, and the EU.

COARSE GRAINS:  The U.S. 1997/98 corn crop is projected at 9.7
billion bushels, down 140 million bushels from last month because of
a reduction in area in the June 30 NASS Acreage report.  Forecast
carryin stocks are up 7 million bushels from last month and domestic
use is up 20 million bushels, leaving ending stocks down 153 million
bushels.  The projected price range for corn is up 5 cents on each end
to $2.30 to $2.70 per bushel. 
Forecast 1996/97 ending stocks of corn are up from last month, as
larger imports and reduced feed and residual use more than offset
higher food, seed, and industrial use. 
Global 1997/98 production is little changed from last month as larger
crops in Russia and the EU offset reductions in the United States and
Canada.  Trade is little changed, but ending stocks are down as the
larger prospective foreign production is expected to be consumed,
while the smaller U.S. crop results in lower stocks.

RICE: U.S. production in 1997/98 is projected at 176 million
hundredweight, up 13.5 million hundredweight from last month
because of a large increase in area reported in the June 30 Acreage
report. Yield is adjusted slightly higher because of a change in the
distribution of area by State and type of rice indicated in the report. 
As a result of the increase in production, imports in 1997/98 are
lowered to 11 million hundredweight, the same as 1996/97.  Domestic
and residual use are raised 1 million hundredweight each from last
month and exports are up 8 million hundredweight.  Ending stocks, at
23.5 million hundredweight, are up nearly 2 million hundredweight
from last month but down slightly from 1996/97.  The season-average
price range for 1997/98 is lowered $0.75 on each end to $9.00-$10.00
per hundredweight. 
Global rice production in 1997/98 is projected at 379 million metric
tons, down 2.1 million tons from last year's record crop but up slightly
from last month's trend-based projection.  World exports and
consumption in 1997/98 are projected higher than a month ago and are
up from 1996/97.  Ending stocks are below last month's level.  With
global consumption projected higher than production, ending stocks
will decline in 1997/98. 

OILSEEDS:  U.S. oilseed production for 1997/98 is projected at a
record 83.2 million tons, up over 2 million tons from last month and
over 8 million tons (11 percent) from 1996/97.  Most of the gain is in
soybeans as projected yields are up slightly and planted area is up 6.6
million acres from last year to 70.9 million acres, the highest since
1982/83.  Large area and production gains are also forecast for
rapeseed.  Small declines are likely for cottonseed and peanut
production, with little change likely for sunflowerseed production.

U.S. soybean crush is projected to reach a record 40 million tons or
1,470 million bushels in 1997/98.  Crush will be helped by another
year of robust growth in domestic soybean meal feeding and by a more
modest gain in prospective soybean meal exports.  Continued high
profitability in poultry feeding and a recovery in hog production are
expected to support record soybean meal use.  Relatively attractive
prices and rising U.S. consumer incomes are expected to result in a
further modest rise in domestic soybean oil use. 
Larger U.S. supplies, lower prices, and strong import demand,
particularly in China and other Asian markets, are expected to
contribute to larger exports of soybean and soybean products in
1997/98.  Soybean exports are projected at 930 million bushels, up 40
million from 1996/97's revised estimate of 890 million bushels. 
Soybean meal and soybean oil exports are projected to rise, but more
slowly than in 1996/97, as increased competition--mainly from South
American soybean production next spring--slows U.S. exports.

Season-average prices for soybeans and products are projected to
decline in 1997/98 as record supplies exceed use gains and ending
stocks for soybeans recover to more normal levels.  Soybean prices are
projected at $5.40 to $6.60 per bushel.  Soybean meal prices are
expected to show the largest relative decline, dropping to $190 to $215
per short ton, with soybean oil off by a lesser degree to 21.0 to 24.5
cents per pound.

Forecast ending stocks for 1996/97 are reduced 5 million bushels this
month as slight reductions in crush and exports are more than offset by
increases in seed use and residual. 
Global oilseed production in 1997/98 is projected at a record 275.3
million tons, up 18.0 million tons from 1996/97 and up 2.3 million
tons from last month.  Soybean production, accounting for most of the
gain, is up 15.0 million tons to a record 146.7 million tons.  Record
crops are expected in South America, Canada, and the United States as
area gains in response to very good grower returns in 1996/97.  Most
of the remaining production gain is in rapeseed, mainly in Canada and
Western Europe. 

SUGAR:  U.S. sugar production in 1997/98 is forecast at 7.575
million short tons, up 75,000 tons from last month and 4.4 percent
over 1996/97.  Beet sugar production in 1997/98 is forecast at 4.3
million short tons, unchanged from last month, but up 6 percent from
last year.  Cane sugar production is forecast to reach 3.275 million
short tons.  Louisiana cane sugar production was raised 75,000 tons
from June indications, to 1.05 million tons.  More producers in that
State have moved to higher yielding varieties following the freeze in
early 1996. 
Slight adjustments to cane sugar production and exports of
sugar-containing products are made for 1996/97.  Production is
estimated at 7,253 million short tons, as production in Puerto Rico is
completed for the year.

LIVESTOCK, POULTRY, AND DAIRY:  The pork production
projection for 1998 is increased slightly.  This summer's pig crop is
expected to be a little larger than previously forecast, so the first
quarter 1998 pork production forecast is increased.  First-half 1997
broiler output was larger than anticipated, boosting the production
estimate for all of 1997.  Beef production this spring fell a little short
of expectations as average weights did not show as much seasonal
increase as forecast.

Beef imports forecasts for both 1997 and 1998 are boosted slightly. 
Most of this increase is because Argentina has been approved to ship
fresh, chilled, or frozen beef to the United States.  Broiler export
prospects have weakened and exports, particularly those to Japan and
Hong Kong, are trailing expected levels.  The forecasts for both 1997
and 1998 are reduced slightly.  On the other hand, with exports of
turkey and mature chicken meat stronger than expected, forecasts for
these categories are increased.  The stronger exports are largely due to
larger shipments to Hong Kong. 

Stocks of dairy products, particularly nonfat dry milk, continue to
exceed expected levels.  The commercial use estimates are lowered as
demand in not measuring up to expectations.  The larger stocks will
keep downward pressure on prices until they are worked lower, so the
milk price forecasts are lowered.  Weaker prices than previously
forecast will hold the growth in milk production below that forecast
last month.

COTTON:  This month's U.S. outlook for 1997/98 includes smaller
anticipated production, exports, and ending stocks.  Production is
revised down 500,000 bales to 18.0 million, reflecting reduced area in
the June 30 Acreage report.  Exports are pegged at 7.1 million bales,
down 200,000 bales from last month based on the reduction in supply,
but about even with 1996/97.  With domestic mill use unchanged at
11.0 million bales, ending stocks of 3.95 million bales are 200,000
bales lower than projected in June.

The world outlook for 1997/98 shows both production and
consumption down less than 1 percent this month. Trade prospects are
revised up slightly to 27.5 million bales.  Ending stocks of 35.2
million bales nearly equal last month's estimate, but are down 1.3
million bales from the beginning level.

For 1996/97, U.S. consumption and exports are revised down slightly,
to 10.85 million and 7.05 million bales, respectively, reflecting recent
activity.  This month's revisions for several foreign countries result in
marginally lower world production and consumption.

Committees.  Committee members are listed on page 30.
