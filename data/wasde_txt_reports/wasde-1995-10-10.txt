COTTON HIGHLIGHTS

Smaller production, use and stocks highlight this
month's changes in the U.S. cotton outlook for 1995/96.
The October 1 crop forecast of 19.1 million bales is 6
percent below a month earlier and 3 percent below last
season's record.  The smaller prospective supply,
relatively high prices and a recent slowdown in textile
activity are having a detrimental impact on total U.S.
cotton use.  This month's mill use is revised down 3
percent to 11.2 million bales and exports are lowered 4
percent to 7.5 million.  The 1995/96 carryover is
projected at 3.2 million bales, 14 percent below the
month-earlier indication but still 21 percent above the
diminutive carryin.

World cotton prospects for 1995/96 also feature
slightly less production, use and stocks.  Global
output is projected at 88.2 million bales, marginally
below a month earlier, as the reduced U.S. crop more
than offsets larger crop forecasts for Pakistan and
Uzbekistan.  Smaller world consumption of 86.4 million
bales reflects downward revisions for the United States
and China.  Global trade prospects remain largely
unchanged.  This season's ending stocks are placed at
31.4 million bales, 1 percent below September
indications but 5 percent above the beginning level.
