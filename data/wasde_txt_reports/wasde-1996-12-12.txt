WASDE-321 - December 12, 1996

WHEAT:  U.S. 1996/97 supply and use projections are unchanged
from last month.  The projected price range is narrowed 10
cents on each end to $4.20 to $4.40 per bushel.

Projected 1996/97 global supply, use, and ending stocks are
little changed from last month.  Larger projected production
for Argentina and Australia and an upward revision in the
official estimate for Canada are largely offset by a reduction
for Kazakstan.  The larger crops result in higher projected
exports for Argentina and additional ending stocks for
Australia.  Projected use and ending stocks are up for Canada
because of the larger crop and reduced exports.  Projected
imports are up 500,000 tons for both Iraq and Turkey, but down
1 million tons for China.

COARSE GRAINS:  Projected 1996/97 U.S. ending stocks of corn
are up 50 million bushels from last month because of lower
forecast exports.  The projected price range for corn is down
10 cents on the high end to $2.50 to $2.80 per bushel.  

Global 1996/97 coarse grain production, use, and stocks are up
from last month, but trade is down.  Larger corn crops in
Brazil and Hungary and higher barley production in Kazakstan
account for most of the increase in global production from last
month.   Projected global imports are down from last month
because of reductions in corn imports by Brazil and Mexico.

RICE: The U.S. rice supply and use forecasts are unchanged from
last month.  
World 1996/97 rice production, consumption, and exports are
down from last month while ending stocks are up.  The smaller
global crop primarily reflects declines in production in
Brazil, India, and Vietnam.  The increase in world rice ending
stocks for 1996/97 results primarily from revisions in supply
and use estimates for Indonesia for 1993/94 through 1996/97. 
The revisions are based in part on official Indonesian rice
stocks data.  A higher 1996/97 export forecast for Vietnam is
more than offset by a reduction for India.  Imports for Brazil
are forecast higher mainly because of the smaller crop.

OILSEEDS:  U.S. oilseed production for 1996/97 is forecast at
75.2 million tons, essentially unchanged from last month but
up 9 percent from last year.  A small gain is indicated for
cottonseed production this month.

The major changes to U.S. trade forecasts this month include
an increase in soybean exports of 30 million bushels to 900
million bushels and an increase of 50,000 tons in sunflowerseed
oil exports, to 320,000 tons.  Upward revisions in China's
soybean and soybean meal imports and higher soybean import
forecasts for the EU, Mexico, and Thailand are helping U.S.
exports make a strong start in 1996/97.  Sunflowerseed oil
exports are being helped by unexpected U.S. sales to India. 
Another notable change this month is a reduction in U.S.
soybean oil extraction rates to 11.1 pounds per bushel, which
lowers production and ending stocks.  Forecast ending soybean
oil stocks remain high at 2.14 billion pounds, second only to
the 2.24 billion in 1991/92.  In contrast, foreign vegetable
oil inventories are forecast to decline in 1996/97 to 5.2
million tons, well below the record high level of 6.2 million
tons set in 1989/90.

U.S. soybean stocks are forecast 30 million bushels lower this
month at 180 million bushels.  Season-average soybean prices
are forecast higher this month at $6.30 to $7.00 per bushel. 
Soybean meal prices are forecast higher at $215 to $235 per
short ton.  Soybean oil prices are raised slightly this month
to 22.0 to 24.0 cents per pound.

Global oilseed production in 1996/97 is forecast at 255.3
million tons, off 2 million tons from last month and down 0.4
million from last year.  A downward revision for soybeans of
1.1 million tons and reduced forecasts for peanuts in China and
sunflowerseed in Russia account for most of the change. 
China's soybean forecast is reduced 0.8 million tons to 12.5
million, based on preliminary indications of less planted area. 
Indonesia's soybean crop is reduced, based on official data. 
India's soybean crop is lowered 0.2 million tons to 4.0 million
tons.

SUGAR:  U.S. sugar production in fiscal year 1996/97 is
forecast at 7.28 million short tons, raw value, up 150,000 tons
from last month.  Beet sugar production is raised to reflect
higher rates of recovery from beets.  In Louisiana, where more
than three-quarters of the sugarcane crop has been processed,
progress to date indicates sugar output will be near 1 million
tons, well above earlier expectations.  Imports under the
tariff rate quota are reduced 70,000 tons to account for an
expected shortfall in shipments by beneficiary countries.

LIVESTOCK, POULTRY, AND DAIRY:  U.S. beef production forecasts
for late 1996 and for 1997 are down this month.  Fourth-quarter
1996 beef production will fall short of the previous forecast
largely because average weights have dropped to lower than
expected levels.  Large placements of cattle in feedlots will
continue into early winter as a result of tight hay supplies,
limited availability of wheat pasture, and lower grain prices. 
But, continued high levels of calf slaughter, large feedlot
placements this fall and winter, and smaller calf crops will
limit placements in 1997 and hold down second-half beef
production.  Cow slaughter, after remaining at high levels
through this winter, is expected to drop sharply below the
year-earlier level in the second half of 1997.  The beef
production forecast for second-half 1997 is reduced and the
Choice steer price forecast is raised.

The pork and poultry supply, use, and price forecasts are
little changed from last month.  The egg price forecasts,
however, are up from previous estimates.  Table egg production
in 1996 is up over 3 percent from the previous year, but egg
prices are being supported by strong export and domestic
demand.  Domestic per capita consumption of eggs is up about
1.3 percent for 1996 and egg exports are up nearly 25 percent. 
Average 1996 egg prices at both the producer and retail levels
are around 20 percent above last year.

Only minor changes are made in the milk supply, use, and price
forecasts.  The 1996/97 estimate of butter removals through the
DEIP is raised from 7 million pounds to 20 million because of
the current weakness in the butter market.


COTTON:  Larger exports and smaller ending stocks highlight
changes in the December U.S. cotton outlook for 1996/97.  
Exports are revised up 7 percent to 6.2 million bales,
reflecting a significant net reduction in competitors'
supplies.  While U.S. mill use is unchanged, production is
forecast slightly higher.   This season's ending stocks are
placed at 4.6 million bales, 6 percent below a month earlier.

On the international scene, this month's 1996/97 outlook
features prospects for both smaller production and consumption,
leaving ending stocks about unchanged from November
indications.  World output is down 1 percent, stemming from
smaller production in Turkmenistan, Uzbekistan, Pakistan,
Brazil, Greece, and Australia.   Slightly smaller global use
reflects a 4-percent cut in China which more than offsets a 6-
percent increase in Pakistan.   World trade prospects are about
unchanged.  This season's ending stocks are forecast 2 percent
above the revised beginning level.
