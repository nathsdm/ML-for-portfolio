COTTON HIGHLIGHTS

Smaller production and use highlight this month's U.S.
cotton outlook for 1995/96.  The November 1 crop
forecast of 18.8 million bales is nearly 2 percent
below a month earlier and 4 percent below last season's
record.  Relatively high cotton prices and a slowdown
in textile activity are continuing to adversely impact
U.S. mill use, which is revised down slightly to 11
million bales.  With intensifying foreign competition,
U.S. exports are lowered 9 percent to 6.8 million
bales.  The 1995/96 carryover is projected at 3.7
million bales, 16 percent above the month-earlier
indication.  Although a million bales above 1994/95,
stocks are projected at only 21 percent of total use.

This month's changes in world cotton prospects for
1995/96 feature larger production, smaller use, and
increased stocks.  Global output is projected at 89.3
million bales, slightly above a month earlier, as
larger crops in Pakistan, China, and India more than
offset smaller production in the United States and
Turkmenistan.  World consumption is shaved to 86
million bales, primarily reflecting reduced U.S. use,
and global trade prospects are also revised down.  This
season's ending stocks are forecast at 32.8 million
bales, 4 percent above October indications and 10
percent above the beginning level.
