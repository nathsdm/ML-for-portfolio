HDR101380000006          WASDE COTTON US & WORLD
                                WASDE-304-25

Note:  This report represents USDA's third assessment
of U.S. and world cotton supply and demand prospects
for the 1995/96 season.  Projections are based on
economic analysis, trends and judgement.  Because
spring planting is still under way in the Northern
Hemisphere and remains several months away in the
Southern Hemisphere, early-season projections are
highly tentative.  Substantial variation may result
from weather developments, economic factors and policy
changes.  For the U.S. crop, the June 30 Acreage report
is used for planted acreage, and methods used to
project harvested acreage and yield are noted in the
table on page 28.


COTTON: The U.S. cotton outlook for 1995/96 has changed
dramatically over the past month.  The 1995 crop is
revised up 2 percent to 21.5 million bales, in line
with larger planted acreage reported June 30.  However,
mill use and export prospects have deteriorated as
recent high cotton prices and tight supplies are
adversely impacting consumption here and abroad, and
stimulating foreign acreage and production.  U.S. mill
use for 1995/96 is revised down 2 percent to 11.4
million bales, while exports are revised down 12
percent to 7.5 million.  These reduced use prospects,
coupled with the larger crop and an upward revision in
beginning stocks, suggest that inventories next season
could rise to 5 million bales, nearly double the month-
earlier projection and more than double the beginning
stock level.

World cotton stocks during 1995/96 also are expected to
rebound more than indicated last month as projected
production exceeds consumption by 4.5 million bales.
Output is pegged at nearly 91 million bales, 2 percent
above last month and 6.5 percent above the 1994 crop.
The United States, Pakistan, Turkey, and the African
Franc Zone countries account for most of the gain from
last season.  Global consumption in 1995/96 is
projected at 86.3 million bales, slightly below a month
ago but 3 percent above a year earlier.  Exports,
projected at nearly 28 million bales in 1995/96, are
off 6 percent from the current season.  Import demand
is expected to slacken in major producing countries--
notably China and Pakistan--both of which encountered
crop problems in 1994/95.  Projected global ending
stocks in 1995/96 of nearly 34 million bales would
equal 39 percent of consumption, up from 35 percent
this season and the 5-year average of 37 percent.
