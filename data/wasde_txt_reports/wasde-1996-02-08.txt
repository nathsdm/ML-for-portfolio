COTTON HIGHLIGHTS

COTTON: The U.S. cotton outlook for 1995/96 is
highlighted this month by an upward revision in
exports which offsets a downward adjustment in
mill use.  Exports are revised up 3 percent to 7.2
million bales, reflecting additional sales to
China and an increased pace of shipments.
However, U.S. mill use is lowered 2 percent to
10.5 million bales as sluggish consumer demand for
textile products continues to adversely impact
cotton consumption.  The 1995/96 carryover is
projected at 3 million bales, unchanged from
January indications and equal to 17 percent of
total use.

This months world cotton prospects for 1995/96
feature a modest decline in both production and
consumption.  The production forecast of 88.6
million bales is down nearly 1 percent this month
with smaller crops estimated for India, Paraguay,
and Egypt.  Consumption is projected at 85.7
million bales, also down nearly 1 percent,
reflecting downward revisions for the United
States, Egypt, and Japan.  World trade is revised
up slightly to about 28 million bales.  This
seasons ending stocks are projected at 31.8
million bales, virtually unchanged from month-
earlier indications and 10 percent above the
beginning level.
