COTTON HIGHLIGHTS

Smaller production, mill use and stocks highlight this
months U.S. cotton outlook for 1995/96.  The December
1 crop forecast of 18.2 million bales is 3 percent
below a month earlier and 7 percent below last seasons
record.  A slowdown in textile activity and continued
relatively high cotton prices are reducing mill
consumption, which is revised down nearly 3 percent to
10.7 million bales.  U.S. exports are unchanged from
last month's estimate of 6.8 million bales.  The
1995/96 carryover is projected at 3.4 million bales, 8
percent below November indications, and equal to 19
percent of total use.

This months changes in world cotton prospects for
1995/96 feature larger trade, along with smaller stocks
stemming primarily from upward revisions in China mill
use during 1993/94 and 1994/95.  Global production this
season of 89.5 million bales is up slightly from last
month as larger production in China, Australia, Turkey
and elsewhere more than offsets smaller crops in the
United States and Brazil.  These production changes
have significant implications for world trade, which is
revised up about 2 percent this month.  Global
consumption of 85.8 million bales is down slightly,
primarily reflecting smaller U.S. use.  This seasons
world ending stocks are forecast at 32.2 million bales,
almost 2 percent below month-earlier indications but 10
percent above the beginning level.
